Q1 — Post Lot Won Funnel
Why don't lot winners request their title after winning the bid?
The Answer in One Sentence
  • 96.3% of lot winners never click Mail My Title because the button is on a different page than the badge, delivery logistics compete for attention immediately, and ~36 users per day hit a broken delivery error page with no recovery.
Key Numbers to Know Cold
  • 69,906 — unique lot winners (badge seers) in 30 days
  • 3.75% — badge-to-title conversion via badge-first pathway (2,556 users)
  • 4,913 — total title requestors across both pathways
  • 6.2 days — median time from badge to title click
  • 59.6% of converters complete within 24h · 88.7% within 7 days · 11.3% take 7+ days
  • 48% of badge-seers (32,819) visit Payment History cross-session — but only 7.59% click Mail Title
  • 92.4% (30,328 users) reach the right page and leave without clicking — hidden button
  • 97% of all conversions flow through Payment History (not Lots Won, 3.8%)
  • 1,087 — users hitting the delivery error page in 30 days (~36/day)
  • 12.5% / 11.2% — top two competing behaviors after badge (browse lots / Lots Won)
Slide 4 — Right-Censoring Callout (Analyst Note)
  • The deck includes a measurement note on the Q1 Answer slide. Do not read it aloud verbatim — use it as a backup if someone challenges the 3.75% rate as low or declining.
  • If they ask "is conversion trending down?": "No — that's a measurement artifact called right-censoring. The 3.75% rate is measured over a 30-day window, but the median journey takes 6.2 days. Members who won lots in the last week of the window didn't have enough time to complete before the measurement closed — so they're counted as non-converters even though many will convert. The underlying daily rate is stable at 3.5–3.8%. This is a structural gap in how conversion is measured, not a declining trend."
  • If they ask why we're showing a rate that undercounts: "Because the rate still represents real friction. 96.3% of lot winners not taking action in 30 days — even accounting for the measurement artifact — is the genuine finding. The artifact explains why the number looks lower than it might be over a longer window, not why it's not a problem."
Talk Points — Slide by Slide
  • Slide 4 (Answer): "The number that matters is 96.3%. Nineteen in twenty lot winners never take the title request step. We're not talking about churn — we're talking about a workflow step that isn't completing."
  • Slide 5 (Journey): "The journey data tells us where they're going. 12.5% are already looking at another lot. 11.2% are managing multiple wins. These aren't confused users — they're busy buyers."
  • Slide 6 (Root Causes): "There's no single cause. The badge and the button live on different pages with no bridge. Delivery logistics are more urgent. And 1,087 members are hitting a dead-end error page every month."
  • Slide 7 (Bug): "This is the finding I want to flag specifically. The delivery error page hits 1,087 unique members a month — 36 a day. The error message says: 'Sorry, shipping is not available for the listed zip code.' That's a valid business constraint. But the page offers nothing else: no transporter directory, no help link, no alternative. A JavaScript exception fires on exit. We reviewed two sessions: a Santa Rosa PREMIER Exporter who hit the error three times in ten seconds and then logged out and back in to retry, and a Freeport desktop user who hit it twice with the exception firing each time. Neither member reached the title step. This is a UX P1 — not because the restriction is wrong, but because the dead end is."
  • Slide 8 (Pathways): "The good news is that 4,913 users found the button — nearly half without touching the badge at all. They went directly to Payment History. These are your experienced buyers who already know the workflow. The lever is the badge-first pathway."
  • Slide 9 (PH Funnel — NEW): "Here's what makes this interesting. 48% of badge-seers — 32,819 members — do make it to Payment History. The button exists there. But 92.4% of them leave without clicking it. This isn't a navigation problem anymore. Something is happening at the page level."
  • Slide 10 (PH Session — NEW): "We watched a PREMIER Dealer in Philadelphia visit Payment History three separate times before clicking. Sessions one and two: they saw 'Mail/Pickup ready' as a status label and left — they thought it meant the title was already being processed. Session three: they clicked the lot row, a detail panel expanded, the button appeared inside, they clicked. 124 seconds. Once they found the entry point, completion was immediate. The friction isn't the flow — it's finding the start."
The Hidden Button — What to Expect from the Room
  • Copart's team will likely know the expand-row interaction exists — they built it. They may push back with "it's discoverable."
  • Counter: "Discoverable to someone who knows to click a row. Not discoverable to someone reading a status label that says 'Mail/Pickup ready' — that reads as confirmation, not as an invitation to take action. The session data shows PREMIER members — your most experienced cohort — making this mistake across multiple sessions."
  • They may ask how many users behave this way vs. the Philadelphia member being an outlier.
  • Counter: "The funnel answer: 30,328 users reach Payment History and don't click. 92.4%. That's not an outlier — that's the default behavior."
  • If they ask why converters convert: experienced buyers who have done this before already know to expand the row. First-time winners don't.
The recommendation you should be ready to give: Two interventions, ranked by impact:

1. Surface the button at list level on Payment History — a "Request Title" button visible in the lot row without expanding fixes the 92.4% miss rate. This is the highest-leverage change available.

2. Add a "Request Title" CTA directly from the You Won badge — eliminates the multi-step navigation barrier for new lot winners. Even a 2× improvement would be ~2,500 additional requests per month.

The delivery error page is a separate P1 fix — recovery CTA needed for the 1,087 users per month stranded with no path forward.

Q1 — Forensic Blind Spots (3 New Findings)
Three issues the prior analysis missed — all newly confirmed with session evidence. Present these after the Tool Chronicle if the audience is technical. Present them after Slide 8 (Pathways) if they're business-focused and you want to keep momentum.
How to Frame These Slides
  • These three slides were not in the original analysis. They were found by re-interrogating the Fullstory data with a forensic lens — testing every claim, then asking "what did we not look for?"
  • Opening line for the room: "After validating the Q1 findings, we ran a second pass asking: what are we missing? Three additional issues surfaced — each confirmed with session replay. I want to walk through them because two are P1s that likely have engineering owners in this room."
  • The three findings form a severity stack: expand failure (mobile, P1, score 15,963) → auth expiry (cross-device, P1, score 13,492) → dead click (desktop, P1, score 4,826). Lead with expand failure — highest impact, most concrete.
Slide NEW-A: Expand Failure (Mobile) — Talk Points
  • Key numbers: 4,093 mobile users blocked · 4.8% of Payment History visitors · priority score 15,963 (highest in Q1)
  • How to open: "Before a member can see the Mail Title button, they have to expand the payment row. For 4,093 mobile members last month — nearly 1 in 21 — that tap throws a JavaScript error. The expand arrow triggers a TypeError that fires within 14 milliseconds. The row partially loads. The invoice details don't. The member taps again. Taps again. Gives up."
  • On the session evidence: "We watched a member in Bloomington on Android go through exactly this. They navigated to Payment History, tapped the expand arrow, got the error, tried Unpaid Invoices instead, opened a transporter scheduling dialog — and never reached the Mail Title button. The session replay is linked in the slide."
  • The trend number matters: "The error rate is up 678% compared to the prior period. This isn't a chronic issue — a recent code change introduced it. That's actually good news: it's likely one commit."
  • 97.6% mobile vs. 2.4% desktop is your diagnostic. Point to it: "This is a touch-event or mobile render issue. Desktop members are almost entirely unaffected."
  • If they ask who owns this: "It's in the expand-row component on the Payment History page — the same row that surfaces the Mail Title button. The TypeError message is: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading '0'). Your front-end team can locate the commit from there."
Slide NEW-B: copart-mail-title Dead Click (Desktop) — Talk Points
  • Key numbers: 1,547 desktop users blocked · +784% frustration rate change · priority score 4,826
  • How to open: "Blind Spot B is the desktop counterpart. 1,547 members found the Mail Title component. It was visible, it looked clickable — and it didn't respond. 2,453 dead click events. Average 1.3 clicks per user before giving up. These aren't discovery failures. These are members who found the interface and the interface failed them."
  • On the session evidence: "We watched a member in Littleton, Windows. They had a lot with Mail/Pickup ready status — lot 5706 2926, a 2019 Subaru Forester in Albuquerque. Payment History loaded correctly. The status label was visible. They clicked the component. Nothing. Dead click recorded."
  • On the +784% comparison: "The frustration rate is up 8.84× versus the prior measurement period. The deck frames this carefully: the comparison baseline reflects a period of lower engagement — not necessarily a period when the component was fully functional. What we can say with confidence is that ~70 dead clicks per day has been the sustained rate for the full 30-day measurement window. This is a chronic pattern, not a single spike. The error rate being up 111% in the same window on the same component points to a state initialization or async binding issue — the click handler isn't resolving before the component renders."
  • If they say the volume is smaller (1,547 vs. 4,093): "True — but these users are further along the workflow. The mobile expand failure blocks access to the row entirely. This blocks the final click on a component they've already found. The two issues compound."
Slide NEW-C: Auth Expiry / 401 Loop — Talk Points
  • Key numbers: 3,697 users affected · 9,258 network errors · 36.7% are 401s · priority score 13,492
  • How to open: "Blind Spot C is an infrastructure issue wearing a UX costume. When a session cookie expires mid-journey, Payment History fires four API calls simultaneously without a valid token. All four return 401. The page renders as 'No records found' — even for members with active lots and title-eligible payments. It looks like an empty page. From the member's perspective, their history disappeared."
  • Walk the sequence: "Here's the exact timeline from a session in Houston. Member was on Unpaid Invoices, navigating to Payment History. 254 milliseconds after the page loads, four calls fire simultaneously: title delivery, e-sign eligibility, license info, invoice data — all 401. Console logs: Error loading title delivery details: status 401. Page shows No records found. Login redirect fires at 59,356ms — 530 milliseconds after they landed. They re-authenticate, go back to Payment History, and try again."
  • The diagnostic detail: "The 1,427 users experiencing 401s on the Login page are the tell. The API is firing during the redirect — before the token refreshes. Payment History needs a session-state guard: don't fire API calls until the auth token is confirmed valid post-redirect."
  • Who this affects: "3,697 users per month. 2.1% of all org users. And the frustration rate is up 112.8%. Members who re-authenticate and return to Payment History still need to complete the Mail Title workflow — now with a second navigational hurdle in front of them."
  • If they say this is an auth system issue, not UX: "The fix is an engineering change — a guard on the API initialization. The impact is UX: members staring at an empty Payment History page with no indication that re-authentication will solve it. The two aren't in conflict — the engineering fix resolves the UX failure."
Priority ranking for the room:

1. Mobile expand failure (Blind Spot A) — fix first. 4,093 users, +678% trend, score 15,963. One commit likely caused it. Engineering can locate it from the TypeError + component selector.

2. Auth expiry 401 loop (Blind Spot C) — add session-state guard. 3,697 users, score 13,492. Do not fire Payment History APIs until auth token is confirmed post-redirect.

3. copart-mail-title dead click (Blind Spot B) — chronic binding investigation. 1,547 users, score 4,826. ~70 dead clicks/day sustained across the 30-day window. Investigate state initialization or async binding on the component — the click handler likely resolves after the component renders. Check for touch-vs-pointer differences; 97.5% of affected users are desktop.

Q1 — Device Behavior (Slide 17)
Cross-device journey and the three broken surfaces that make mobile a dead end for title requests.
The Counterintuitive Finding
  • Mobile is 3.5× overrepresented among title clickers (36% of clicks, 10% of badge views) — yet every sampled funnel converter completed on Desktop. The reason: all three surfaces where the title CTA exists or is blocked are broken on mobile.
Device Breakdown — Numbers to Know
  • Desktop: 61,602 badge views · 1,098 title clicks · 1.78% conversion
  • Mobile: 6,886 badge views · 609 title clicks · 8.84% conversion (but 36% of all clicks)
  • Payments are ~70% mobile · All sampled converters completed on Desktop
  • Median journey: 6.2 days from badge to title request
Cross-Device Journey — Talk Points
  • Opening frame: "Before we go to Q2, I want to show you something the device data reveals about the title request journey. Mobile and Desktop are playing different roles — and the data explains why conversion is almost entirely happening on Desktop even though mobile drives payments."
  • On the journey: "Here's the pattern. Members win on Desktop — that's where the You Won badge fires, in auction view. Payment happens on mobile — your payments are 70% mobile. After paying, they land on the payment success page. And then nothing. No title CTA. That's the dead end. An email arrives days later — AuctionReminder or Buyer Pickup Complete — they open it, go back to Desktop, navigate to Payment History, and eventually find the Mail Title button. The median gap is 6.2 days. Email is what bridges the device switch."
  • The three broken surfaces: "(1) Payment success page — 29,169 monthly visitors, 'Arrange transportation' is the only CTA. No title mailing option. (2) Dashboard 'Get titles (N)' tab — ChunkLoadError: Loading chunk 2368 failed. Members who navigate there expecting to manage title requests hit a JavaScript error. (3) Mobile Payment History expand — the Typography expand tap that would reveal the Mail Title button throws a TypeError for 4,093 mobile users per month. So even members who navigate to the right page on mobile can't access the button."
  • Why this matters for the fix list: "Fixing any one of these surfaces routes mobile members to Desktop less. Fixing the payment success page — adding a title CTA there — captures members at peak intent, immediately after they've paid. That's the highest-leverage intervention."
  • If they ask why mobile has a higher click-through rate despite lower volume: "The 8.84% rate on mobile vs. 1.78% on desktop is the wrong comparison — mobile badge seers are more engaged members by selection. The insight is that despite higher intent, mobile title clicks don't translate to funnel completions. All the mechanical barriers are on mobile."

Q2 — Time to First Bid
How many days does it take for a new active member to place their first bid?
The Answer in One Sentence
  • 4.74 days median — but the distribution is trimodal: 27% bid within 24 hours, 41% bid within 7 days, and 32% take up to 90 days. If they haven't bid by day 7, most won't.
Key Numbers to Know Cold
  • 298,666 — new registrants in 90-day window
  • 71 — first bids placed in that window (2.4% activation)
  • 4.74 days / 409,178,480ms — median registration-to-bid
  • 27% — same-day bidders (19 users, median 64 min)
  • 68% — bid within 7 days (48 users)
  • 32% — long-tail (days 8–90, 23 users)
  • Page 1310 — Complete Registration proxy for new member
  • 6N30kCdfTpDV — Bid Now Click defined event ID
Talk Points — Slide by Slide
  • Slide 13 (Answer): "4.74 days is the median. But the median alone doesn't tell the story — it's masking a three-part distribution. Let me show you."
  • Slide 14 (Methodology): "Before we built any funnel, we watched a real first-bid session. That's not typical analytics. We do this because the session teaches us how to build the funnel correctly."
  • Slide 15 (Distribution): "The three cohorts have different implications. The 27% who bid same-day are high-intent — they came to bid on a specific vehicle. The 41% who bid within 7 days are your re-engagement target. The 32% long-tail will convert on their own schedule — campaigns have low marginal yield there."
  • Slide 16 (Session): "The Gilroy session is exactly why we watch before we aggregate. 25 minutes of photo inspection, reading condition reports, checking lot details — then the bid. The 4.74-day median makes sense when you understand the due diligence first-time buyers do."
